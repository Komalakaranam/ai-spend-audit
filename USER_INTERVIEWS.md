# User Research Notes

## Overview

The project idea was influenced by observing how students, developers, and small teams increasingly rely on multiple AI tools simultaneously without clear visibility into total monthly subscription costs.

The goal of this research was to understand:
- how users manage AI subscriptions
- whether users track spending actively
- common pain points around AI tooling costs

---

# Informal User Discussions

## Student Developers

Observed behaviors:
- subscribing to multiple AI tools simultaneously
- overlapping functionality between tools
- limited awareness of cumulative monthly spending
- experimentation with premium plans without long-term evaluation

Common tools mentioned:
- ChatGPT
- GitHub Copilot
- Cursor
- Claude

---

## Small Team Use Cases

Observed concerns:
- difficulty deciding which AI subscriptions are necessary
- confusion around pricing tiers
- duplicated spending across teams
- uncertainty about seat utilization

---

# Key Insights

## Insight 1

Many users underestimate total AI subscription costs because spending is distributed across multiple platforms.

---

## Insight 2

Users often keep subscriptions active even when usage decreases.

---

## Insight 3

Most users lack a centralized view of AI tooling expenses.

---

## Insight 4

Simple optimization recommendations are easier for users to understand than highly technical analytics dashboards.

---

# Product Decisions Influenced by Research

The following product decisions were influenced by these observations:
- simplified audit workflow
- instant savings calculations
- shareable reports
- lightweight onboarding
- rule-based recommendations

---

# Limitations

This research was informal and observation-based rather than large-scale structured interviews.

Future iterations could include:
- survey-based research
- startup founder interviews
- usage analytics
- pricing sensitivity studies
- enterprise workflow analysis
